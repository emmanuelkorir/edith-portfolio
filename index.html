02 / Approach

Four moves, repeated.

Every project starts with the same discipline: understand the place, work with the climate, design for other people, then resolve the details that let a building age well.

  1. 01

    Read the site

    Walk it, measure it, watch where the water goes and where people already gather. The best move is often already there, waiting to be noticed.

    Orientation · Light · Ground
  2. 02

    Design with climate

    Shade before air-conditioning, breeze before fans, planting before screens. Comfort is a spatial decision long before it is a mechanical one.

    Shade · Air · Water
  3. 03

    Make room for others

    Buildings are shared before they are photographed. I design thresholds, edges and in-between spaces for the people who will use them every day.

    Thresholds · Belonging · Public life
  4. 04

    Detail for time

    Choose materials that weather honestly and assemblies that can be repaired. A good detail is one that still makes sense in twenty years.

    Material · Craft · Repair

03 / About

A practice of listening.

Edith Nduta Kuria is an architect based in Nairobi, Kenya. She graduated from Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology in 2025 with a Bachelor of Architecture, after six years of study, and practises at Locus Studio.

Her work moves between residential, civic and adaptive-reuse projects, with a consistent interest in climate, material honesty and the social life of buildings. She is drawn to projects that begin with a constraint — a tight plot, a tired structure, a shifting shoreline — and find generosity inside it.

At Locus Studio she works across concept design, drawing sets and coordination, continuing a studio culture of careful observation and quiet, buildable detail.

“Good architecture doesn’t announce itself. It makes daily life feel inevitable.”
